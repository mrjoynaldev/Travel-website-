# SKILL: Social Proof — Video Reviews & Food Menu

Travellers don't believe operators; they believe other travellers and food
photos. This skill runs the two showcase engines: **video reviews**
(`/studio/reviews` → homepage `#stories`) and **food menu**
(`/studio/food-menu` → homepage `#food`). Both stay hidden until published —
an empty showcase beats a fake one.

Companions: `LEAD-SKILL.md` (why proof converts) · `TOUR-SKILL.md` (honesty).

---

## 1. Collecting video reviews (the routine that matters)

**When:** at the END of every good trip, before goodbyes — happiness decays
fast. Make it a crew habit, not a campaign.

**How to ask (owner/crew script):**
> "We're glad you loved it! Would you record 20 seconds on my phone — just
> your name, where you're from, and one honest line about the trip? It helps
> the next family decide. Only if you're comfortable — and tell me if you
> want it taken down anytime."

**Rules — non-negotiable:**
1. **Real customers, real consent.** Verbal on camera is fine ("can we share
   this?"). Anyone can ask for removal anytime → remove within 24h, no debate.
2. **Never fabricate.** No staff pretending to be tourists, no scripted lines,
   no paid actors, no stock clips. One exposed fake destroys the brand.
3. **Keep it raw.** 15–60 seconds, phone quality is perfect. Over-edited
   reviews look like ads and convert worse.
4. **What to capture:** name + city, which tour, ONE specific detail (the
   sunrise cruise, the fish curry, the guide's bird call). Specifics persuade;
   "it was awesome" does not.
5. **Upload path:** Studio → Video reviews → tap-to-upload (≤50 MB) → quote in
   their exact words → rating only if they stated one → Publish.

**Cadence:** 2–4 new reviews per month in season. Recency sells — a 2024 clip
wall in 2026 looks abandoned. Retire nothing; order newest first.

## 2. Food photography (the silent converter)

Families — the core segment — decide heavily on food. Rules:

1. **Photograph what the kitchen actually serves** on tours and stays. The
   plate in the photo must be reproducible tomorrow.
2. **Shoot the real spread:** fish curry + rice + dal + veg + sweets looks like
   abundance; a single artful bowl looks like scarcity.
3. **Light + context:** daylight, on the boat table or resort spread. No dark
   kitchen snaps, no menu-card photos from other restaurants.
4. **Caption honestly:** dish name + where it's served ("Lunch on the boat",
   "Dinner at the resort"). Price note = "Included in 2D/1N" where true.
5. **Upload path:** Studio → Food menu → tap-to-upload photo (required) →
   category → Publish. 8–12 live dishes is a full gallery; add seasonal
   specials (hilsa in monsoon, winter picnic items) as they come.

## 3. Publishing rhythm

- Reviews and dishes go live as **Draft first, Publish after a second look**
  (faces, names, audio clarity, no visible phone numbers or kids without
  parental consent on camera).
- Homepage shows newest first — no manual curation needed beyond ordering.
- Monthly: check both sections on a real phone. Slow video or broken image =
  fix the same day.

## 4. What NEVER to do

- Fake reviews, bought reviews, "review in exchange for discount" scripts.
- Blurring reality: old photos presented as current, other operators' food.
- Showing customer phone numbers, booking IDs, or children without explicit
  parental consent.
- More than ~6 reviews / ~8 dishes on the homepage — rotate, don't stack.
  Archives can hold the rest (future feature; for now keep the best live).
